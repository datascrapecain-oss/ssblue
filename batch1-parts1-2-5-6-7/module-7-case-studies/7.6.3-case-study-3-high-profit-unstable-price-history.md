# Case Study 3: High Profit, Unstable Price History

At first glance this product appears almost flawless. Sales volume is extremely strong, demand is obvious, competition looks manageable, and the margin at the current Buy Box price is attractive. On paper, this looks like an easy decision.

The problem isn't the product. It's the price context.

## Why This Product Is So Convincing

All the headline metrics line up in a way that naturally builds confidence. Estimated sales are high, the category is familiar, and the profit calculation works comfortably at the current Buy Box price. There are no obvious red flags in eligibility or compliance, and demand clearly exists. If analysis stops here, this product would almost certainly be purchased.

## What the Charts Reveal When You Slow Down

Looking beyond the current price, the charts tell a different story. The Buy Box price has risen sharply in a short period and now sits well above the level it held for most of the previous months. The increase isn't gradual and doesn't reflect a long-term pricing trend.

This kind of movement usually indicates a temporary imbalance: sellers running out of stock, short-term scarcity, a recent demand spike, or a delay before new sellers re-enter. The key issue is that the current price isn't yet proven.

## Why This Is Easy to Miss

Tools surface the current Buy Box price prominently, and profit calculations are naturally anchored to that number. When sales are strong, it's easy to assume the price is justified and sustainable.

But prices that rise quickly can fall just as quickly. Without time to establish a new baseline, there's no guarantee the current margin will still exist once inventory arrives and competition normalises. This is where many sellers make expensive mistakes, buying based on what just happened, not what usually happens.

## Why This Fails for Now

This product doesn't fail because it's bad. It fails because the margin relies on a price that hasn't stabilised. Buying at this point means betting the new price will hold, rather than confirming that it already has. For a new seller, that's unnecessary risk. The correct response here isn't rejection forever. It's restraint.

## Core Lesson

A great product at the wrong price is still a bad buy. Price context matters as much as price itself. When a Buy Box price has moved sharply and recently, treat it with caution until it proves it can hold. The easiest mistakes to make are often the quiet ones, the deals that look too clean. This is a product to monitor, not rush.
