# Case Study 3: High Profit, Unstable Price History

At first glance this product appears almost flawless. Buy cost is £13, the current Buy Box price is £23.99, giving a profit of roughly £4.90 a unit at a 38% ROI, both well past the Gate 1 and Gate 2 floors. Estimated sales are running at 210 units a month, comfortably past the Gate 3 floor. On paper, this looks like an easy decision.

The problem isn't the product. It's the price context.

## Why This Product Is So Convincing

All the headline metrics line up in a way that naturally builds confidence. Estimated sales are high, the category is familiar, and the profit calculation works comfortably at the current Buy Box price. There are no obvious red flags in eligibility or compliance, and demand clearly exists. If analysis stops at Gate 3, this product would almost certainly be purchased.

## What the Charts Reveal When You Slow Down (Gate 4)

Looking beyond the current price, the charts tell a different story. Fourteen days ago, the Buy Box price was £15.49. It's now £23.99, a rise of roughly 55% inside a 30-day window, past the 50% swing this Blueprint treats as the Gate 4 ceiling. The increase isn't gradual and doesn't reflect a long-term pricing trend.

This kind of movement usually indicates a temporary imbalance: sellers running out of stock, short-term scarcity, a recent demand spike, or a delay before new sellers re-enter. The key issue is that the current price isn't yet proven.

## Why This Is Easy to Miss

Tools surface the current Buy Box price prominently, and profit calculations are naturally anchored to that number. When sales are strong, it's easy to assume the price is justified and sustainable.

But prices that rise quickly can fall just as quickly. Without time to establish a new baseline, there's no guarantee the current margin will still exist once inventory arrives and competition normalises. This is where many sellers make expensive mistakes, buying based on what just happened, not what usually happens.

## Why This Fails Gate 4, for Now

This product doesn't fail because it's bad, it clears Gates 1 through 3 comfortably. It fails Gate 4 because the margin relies on a price swing that hasn't stabilised. Buying at this point means betting the new price will hold, rather than confirming that it already has. For a new seller, that's unnecessary risk. The correct response here isn't rejection forever, it's Park / Monitor: revisit once the price has held for another 30 days.

## Core Lesson

A great product at the wrong price is still a bad buy. Price context matters as much as price itself. When a Buy Box price has moved more than the Gate 4 threshold in a short window, treat it with caution until it proves it can hold. The easiest mistakes to make are often the quiet ones, the deals that look too clean. This is a product to monitor, not rush.
