# Case Study 8: Passes Today, Fails Tomorrow

At first glance this product looks acceptable. Current pricing supports profit, ROI clears basic thresholds, and demand appears healthy in isolation. There are no immediate red flags in eligibility, competition, or fees. Assessed purely on a single-day snapshot, it would pass the majority of gates without resistance.

## Historical Behaviour Tells a Different Story

Looking beyond the current state reveals the real risk. Historical pricing shows sharp drops driven by seasonal demand and promotional activity, followed by abrupt price recoveries once demand collapses. These patterns indicate that the current Buy Box price is inflated relative to the product's long-term behaviour, not sustainably earned.

## Seasonal Demand and Timing Risk

This is a classic seasonal item. Demand spikes around a narrow window, sellers flood the listing, prices compress, and then collapse once the season ends. The current price uplift exists only because supply has temporarily thinned. As soon as new sellers restock, Amazon intervenes, or demand normalises, margin erosion is almost guaranteed.

## Why This Is Easy to Miss

Tools show what's happening now, not why it's happening. Without reading the historical context, this product appears stable and profitable. Beginners often mistake temporary recovery phases for genuine stability, entering just as the next price drop is imminent.

## Core Lesson

Passing analysis doesn't mean "safe," it means "not obviously bad right now." Seasonal and historically volatile products can pass every gate today and still fail tomorrow. This is why experience, context, and restraint matter more than excitement. A clean snapshot is a starting point, not a guarantee.
