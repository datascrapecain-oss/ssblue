# Case Study 6: Death by Competition

At first glance this product looks workable. The margins are acceptable at the current Buy Box price, demand is clearly present, and there are no immediate compliance or restriction issues. For a beginner, this is exactly the kind of listing that feels "safe": established brand, regular sales, a price point that leaves room for profit on paper. This is where many sellers stop their analysis.

## What Actually Kills the Product

The problem isn't demand or pricing. It's seller density and behaviour. The offer count is high, stock levels are uneven, and pricing is constantly adjusted in small increments. Sellers are repeatedly undercutting each other by pennies, not because demand requires it, but because competition pressure forces it.

This creates a race-to-the-bottom environment where profit exists only briefly. Any margin visible today is fragile and can disappear the moment one seller decides to clear stock, match a lower landed cost, or accept thinner returns for volume. Over time, this compresses pricing until the product becomes unworkable for most sellers.

## Core Lesson

Even with demand and acceptable margins, excessive competition erodes pricing power. Products like this aren't inherently bad, but they're unstable, short-lived, and unforgiving, especially for newer sellers without cost advantages or fast exit strategies.
