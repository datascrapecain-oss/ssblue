# 6.3 Using a SourceSheet in Practice: Reading a Row Correctly

### The quick actions on every row

Four icons sit at the end of each row:

* **Add to Buy List**: shortlists the product without leaving the sheet
* **View on Amazon**: opens the live listing in a new tab
* **View on Supplier Site**: opens the product on the supplier's own site, so you can check stock and price yourself
* **Open in ASIN Analyser**: jumps straight into the full single-product breakdown from Module 5

Get comfortable with these before you need them. You'll use "Add to Buy List" constantly once you're actually sourcing.

### Opening the full detail

Click anywhere on a product's name or image and a detail view opens with everything you need to make a real decision:

* **Product Match**: the supplier's product image and Amazon's listing image side by side, with a match confidence percentage. Always glance at this first. A low match percentage means SourceSheets isn't fully sure it's found the right listing, don't trust the numbers below it until you've checked by eye.
* **Pricing & Profitability**: Buy Cost, Sale Price, Net Profit, ROI, Margin, and Break Even, all recalculating live if you adjust the Buy Cost field.
* **Recommendation & Risk**: a plain-language verdict ("Worth considering if bought below £X"), Estimated Sales, a Confidence rating, IP Risk, and whether the product is flagged for Hazmat/DG.
* **Keepa Price History**: the full price and sales rank chart, not just the row's thumbnail.
* **Profit by Price Tier**: how profit and ROI would look at the current price versus the 30, 60, 90, and 180 day averages. A current price sitting well above its own averages is worth a second look before you buy.
* **Other Suppliers**: every other supplier SourceSheets tracks that also carries this exact ASIN, with their cost and the profit difference. Check this before buying, sometimes a better deal on the same product sits with a different supplier entirely.

### Reading a row correctly, in practice

Don't evaluate on Profit or ROI alone. A high ROI on a product with 1 estimated sale a month, or 15 competing sellers, isn't a real opportunity. It's a number that happens to look good in isolation. Read Demand and Sellers alongside Profit and ROI every time, and open the full detail before you add anything to your Buy List for real.
