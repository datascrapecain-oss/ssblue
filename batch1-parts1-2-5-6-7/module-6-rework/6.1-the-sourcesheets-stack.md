# 6.1 The SourceSheets Stack

Your left-hand menu has three groups. Here's what each one is for.

### General

* **Dashboard**: your home screen. Active Suppliers, Profitable Today, Opportunities Today, your Buy List count, tracked storefronts, and monitored ASINs, plus your recent analyses and a "Picked for you" preview.
* **Picked for you**: a personalised feed, marked Beta, built from your Buy List and buying pattern. Each suggestion carries a SourceScore out of 100 and a short reason ("from a supplier you buy from often", "category you frequently source"). Thin when your account is new, gets sharper the more you use SourceSheets.
* **Sourcing Library**: browse suppliers directly, split into **Retail Arbitrage**, **Online Arbitrage**, **Wholesale**, and **Categories**. Use this when you want to work through a specific retailer's full product range, or browse an entire product category across every tracked supplier.
* **Radar**: a single live feed aggregating every enabled supplier sheet in your region, already narrowed to your saved Minimum ROI and Minimum Sales preferences. No setup, no picking a supplier first. For most sellers, this becomes the actual daily starting point.
* **Analyse**: upload your own supplier price list (CSV or XLSX) and SourceSheets matches it against Amazon listings for you. Use this for a supplier SourceSheets doesn't already track.
* **Buylist**: your shortlist. Every product you've added gets bucketed automatically into Strong Opportunity, Need Review, or Rejected (more on this in 6.4), with running totals for cost, profit, and ROI across everything on it.

### Tools

* **Storefront Stalker**: track a competitor's Amazon storefront and see everything else they sell profitably, with the same profit and ROI breakdown as a supplier sheet.
* **ASIN Monitor**: track specific ASINs over time and get alerted when price, competition, or eligibility crosses a threshold you set.
* **ASIN Analyser**: look up one specific ASIN on demand (covered in Module 5).

### What to focus on first

Don't try to use all of this on day one. Start with **Radar** to get a feel for what "profitable" actually looks like across a wide range of products, then move into **Sourcing Library** once you want to work a specific retailer or category in depth. Everything else earns its place as you go.
