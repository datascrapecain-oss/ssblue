# Phase 5: Risk Flags & Hard Stops (Gate 7+)

By the time a product reaches this phase, it has already passed the most obvious filters. The maths works, demand is understandable, competition is survivable. This is where many sellers relax, and where some of the most damaging mistakes happen.

Phase 5 identifies non-negotiable risks. These aren't issues you optimise around or revisit later, they're conditions that override everything else. A product that fails here is rejected immediately, regardless of how strong it looked earlier.

**Why risk flags are treated differently.** Earlier gates assess viability. This gate assesses exposure. Some risks can't be priced in, managed with experience, or mitigated with better execution. They sit outside your control and can affect account health, inventory eligibility, and long-term selling rights. This phase is deliberately conservative.

## Gate 7+: Risk Flags & Hard Stops

Unlike earlier phases, this isn't a single gate. It's a collection of hard stops. Any one of them is enough to end analysis.

**Alerts and warnings.** Analysis tools surface alerts for a reason. Not every warning is catastrophic, but certain alerts should never be ignored, especially early on: high-risk warnings, historical account health issues, suppression-related alerts, known listing instability flags. If an alert indicates a problem you can't directly resolve, the product fails here.

**Hazmat and compliance.** Not beginner-friendly problems. Products flagged for hazmat review, safety documentation, restricted shipping, or special handling requirements often involve delays, additional costs, or outright listing blocks. Even if a product looks profitable, compliance uncertainty makes outcomes unpredictable. Hazmat is treated as a hard stop.

**Restrictions and brand behaviour.** Restrictions aren't always permanent, but brand behaviour matters. Some brands actively remove sellers, enforce distribution tightly, reject ungating attempts consistently, or trigger frequent listing removals. If a brand shows a clear pattern of hostility toward resellers, avoid it regardless of other signals. This gate is about recognising patterns, not arguing with them.

**When "maybe" still means no.** This is the most important rule of Phase 5. If you find yourself thinking "it might be fine," "others seem to be selling it," "I'll test it with a small quantity," or "I'll deal with it if it happens," that's the signal to stop. Uncertainty at this stage isn't opportunity. It's exposure.

## Outcome of Phase 5

Only two outcomes exist: fail, if a hard stop is present, or proceed, if no unresolved risk flags are detected. There's no monitoring, parking, or testing at this stage. Passing Phase 5 simply means the product is clear of known structural risks at the time of analysis.
