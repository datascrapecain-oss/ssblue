# 7.3 Understanding Ungating: What It Is, When It Matters, and How to Approach It Correctly

## Do You Even Need This?

Most Retail Arbitrage and Online Arbitrage sellers never bother with formal ungating. If a product or brand shows as restricted, the simplest move is to skip it and find the next opportunity, there are always more ungated products to sell. Treat this page as optional reading for when you specifically want into a gated brand or category, not as a required step before you can start selling.

## 7.3.1 What is Ungating?

When you start selling on Amazon, you'll notice that not every product is open for you to list. Some categories, sub-categories, and brands are restricted, meaning Amazon won't let you sell them until you've proven you're a legitimate, trustworthy seller. Getting that approval is what sellers call ungating.

Amazon uses gating as quality control. They want reliable sellers with genuine supply chains listing products in sensitive areas like toys, groceries, beauty, and health, protecting customers from counterfeits and poor-quality goods, and protecting brands from unauthorised resellers.

For you, ungating is about unlocking opportunity you'd otherwise walk past. Entire categories like Grocery or Beauty are gated by default. Major brands like Lego, Disney, Funko, or Philips are gated to reduce counterfeit risk. Certain sub-niches (hazmat, topical creams, DVDs, pet supplies) are locked down for safety and compliance reasons.

Ungating isn't a one-time thing. It's a process some sellers revisit as they scale and want access to a specific gated brand. Every approval gives you access to new products, more profit potential, and more flexibility in sourcing, but it's an optional unlock, not a prerequisite for selling.

## 7.3.2 How Ungating Works

Ungating follows a fairly standard process, though the details vary by brand or category.

**Step 1: Discover the restriction.** You'll usually find out a product is gated when you try to add it in Seller Central. Amazon shows a message like "You need approval to sell this brand." From there you can click through to apply.

**Step 2: Get an invoice that meets Amazon's bar.** To prove you're a legitimate seller, you need an invoice, not a till receipt, showing at least 10 units of the product, your business name and address matching your Amazon Seller Account exactly, and dated within the last 180 days. A normal retail purchase doesn't produce this kind of paperwork, which is why ungating usually means a separate, one-off purchase from a business-facing seller rather than your everyday RA or OA buying. Wholesale suppliers are the most common route to this, covered in Module 14 if you decide to pursue it.

**Step 3: Submit the invoice.** Upload it through Seller Central as part of your ungating request. Amazon checks the document automatically, and in many cases a human reviewer looks it over too.

**Step 4: Wait for review.** Approval can come back in hours or take several days. Sometimes you'll get an auto-rejection (a standard template email), other times a rejection that specifies why. Persistence matters here.

**Step 5: Get approved and start selling.** Once Amazon accepts your invoice, your account is ungated for that brand or category and you can list and sell freely.

Ungating isn't always about the exact product you bought. Getting ungated in a brand often opens the entire brand catalogue, and getting ungated in a category like Grocery can unlock many brands within it.

## 7.3.3 Strategy & Tips from Sellers

If you decide a gated brand is worth unlocking, ungating isn't just buying 10 units and submitting an invoice. It's a game of persistence and preparation.

**Persistence is everything.** Invoices commonly get rejected multiple times even when everything looks correct. Many sellers only succeed after resubmitting the same invoice 5 to 7 times, different reviewers make different decisions.

**Buy the minimum.** Amazon typically requires 10 units on an invoice, though some brands ask for 100. Start with 10 when possible to minimise cost while still qualifying.

**Match your details exactly.** Your invoice must perfectly match your Seller Central account: business name, registered address, contact details. Even small differences (e.g. "Street" vs "St.") can trigger rejection.

**Resell vs return.** Once approved, you can either sell the items (even at break-even or a slight loss) or return them to the supplier if allowed. The goal isn't profit here. It's unlocking the category.

**Retail and till receipts don't count.** Amazon requires a proper invoice, from a wholesaler, distributor, or business account with a VAT invoice, not a receipt from a supermarket or high-street shop. This is the one point in the Blueprint where you'll need something beyond ordinary RA/OA buying.

**Timing and reviewers matter.** Some rejections come down to which reviewer you get. If your invoice is correct, wait 24 to 48 hours and try again.

**One invoice, multiple wins.** A single invoice can sometimes unlock multiple brands or categories at once, buying branded toothbrushes from a wholesale pharmacy might unlock several oral care brands together.

## 7.3.4 Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I ungate with retail stores like Tesco or Boots?** No. Retail receipts won't work, Amazon only accepts proper invoices, not till receipts. This is why ungating sits outside normal RA/OA buying.

**Can I use online shops?** Only if they provide a proper VAT invoice rather than an order confirmation. Many wholesalers have online portals that work fine for this.

**How many units do I need?** Most categories require 10 units. Some higher-risk brands may require 100.

**Can I return the items?** Yes, many sellers do. Amazon may verify orders with suppliers though, so returning too often can increase your risk.

**Do I need to buy expensive products?** No. Many sellers choose the cheapest item in a brand or category just to meet the 10-unit requirement.

**How long does ungating take?** Anywhere from a few hours to a week. Rejections and resubmissions can extend this.

**Will I always be approved?** Not always, some brands are strict. But most categories can be unlocked with persistence.

**Does ungating last forever?** Yes. Once approved, your account keeps that access, you don't need to repeat the process for the same brand or category.

## 7.3.5 Final Tips for New Sellers

Ungating can feel like a frustrating barrier, but for most new sellers it's simply optional, there's no shortage of ungated products to build your business on first. Treat it as something to come back to once you've found a specific gated brand worth unlocking, not a box to tick before you start.

If and when you do pursue it, don't give up after the first rejection, and start with cheap, branded items to unlock bigger categories. It's not just about approvals. It's about proving yourself as a serious seller with the paperwork Amazon trusts, on the specific occasions that requires it.
