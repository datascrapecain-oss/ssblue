# SourceSheets Blueprint — Style Guide (AI-tell purge)

Apply this pass across all existing pages (Parts 1-4) once ready. Part 5's new draft already follows it.

## Cut on sight

- **Em dashes.** Replace with a comma, a full stop, or a colon depending on the beat. Never bridge two clauses with "—".
- **Rule-of-three lists in prose** ("clear, structured, and proven" / "faster, easier, and safer"). Real writing doesn't stack three adjectives every sentence. Say the one that matters.
- **"It's not just X, it's Y."** Banned construction, appears constantly in AI output, never appears in how a person actually explains something over coffee.
- **Throat-clearing openers**: "Let's dive in," "Here's the thing," "In today's fast-paced world of...," "Now that we've covered X, let's move to Y." Just start the sentence with the content.
- **Manufactured urgency/hype words**: "game-changer," "unlock," "seamless," "effortless," "supercharge," "elevate," "unleash." SourceSheets already has a confident, plain voice — let the facts carry the weight, not the adjectives.
- **Bulleted lists standing in for an actual sentence.** A list is for genuinely parallel, scannable items (settings, steps). It is not a substitute for explaining something in a paragraph. If every section is bullets, nothing reads like a person wrote it.
- **Restating the heading in the first sentence.** ("In this section, we'll cover how to set up your Revolut account.") Just start explaining.
- **Excessive bolding.** Bold is for the one or two things a skimming reader needs, not every noun phrase.
- **Hedging filler**: "It's worth noting that," "It's important to remember," "Keep in mind that." Say the thing directly.

## Keep / lean into

- Second person, direct address ("you," "your business"), which the book already does well.
- Short, declarative sentences. Plain full stops over comma splices.
- The book's real personality: blunt reassurance ("no pressure, no hidden conditions"), a slightly dry, older-sibling tone, willingness to say "most products should fail" rather than softening it.
- Concrete numbers over vague claims. "£25 for the first month" beats "an affordable monthly fee."

## Mechanical check before publishing a page

1. Search the page for "—" and remove every instance.
2. Read it aloud. Anywhere you'd naturally pause and rephrase if a colleague were saying it to you, rewrite it.
3. Cut the first sentence if it's just restating the title.
4. If a paragraph has more than one "it's not just... it's..." or triple-adjective list, rewrite from scratch rather than patch it.
